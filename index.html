01 / The journey

No straight line.
Just the next thing to learn.

I didn’t begin with a perfect roadmap. I got better by making things, running into problems, and staying with them until they made sense.

01

The beginning

Curiosity before credentials.

I’m self-taught. The browser became my classroom: inspect how a page works, try an idea, break something, and figure out why. Progress came from practice, not from waiting to feel ready.

LEARN BY DOING
02

The shift

Make a thing someone can use.

A build changes when it leaves your laptop. A checkout flow has to make sense. A page needs to load. A login bug needs to stay fixed. The small details stop being small when another person depends on them.

FROM DEMO TO DELIVERY
03

The practice

Use better tools. Keep the judgment.

AI tools can help explore options, explain unfamiliar code, and shorten repetitive work. They can also be confidently wrong. I treat the output as a draft to inspect, test, and understand before it becomes part of a product.

AI ASSISTS. I DECIDE.
04

The direction

Build with people, not just for a brief.

Freelancing is more than shipping a page. It means asking clearer questions, making trade-offs visible, sharing progress, and leaving someone with software they can actually use.

GOOD WORK, CLEARLY SHARED
↳

The thread through all of it: understand the problem, make a working version, test it, then improve what matters.

02 / AI in the workflow

Move faster.
Don’t switch off your brain.

I use AI as a practical tool in the development process—not as a substitute for understanding the product or checking the result.

A / 01

Explore the options

Compare approaches, map unfamiliar concepts, and turn a vague question into a testable plan.

A / 02

Get to a first draft

Use AI to help with scaffolding, repetitive code, edge-case lists, or an explanation when stuck.

A / 03

Check the actual behaviour

Read the code, challenge assumptions, test real flows, and fix the parts that do not hold up.

A / 04

Own what ships

The finished work needs to be understandable, maintainable, and useful—not merely generated.

04 / How I work

01

Start with the problem

Before picking a stack, I want to know who the site is for, what they need to do, and what success should look like.

02

Make progress visible

Small milestones, working previews, and honest updates beat a big reveal at the end. Feedback is part of the build, not a final ceremony.

03

Ship, then stay curious

Test the important paths, pay attention to the rough edges, and improve the parts that make a real difference to the person using it.
